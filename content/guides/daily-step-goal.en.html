There is no single magic number. In a 2022 analysis that pooled 15 studies and 47,471 adults, people who took more steps a day had a lower risk of death from any cause. The benefit leveled off at about 6,000 to 8,000 steps a day for adults aged 60 and over, and at about 8,000 to 10,000 steps for younger adults.

Is 10,000 steps a day the target?

10,000 is a round number, not a threshold found in research. In the same analysis, younger adults reached the leveling-off point somewhere between 8,000 and 10,000 steps, and older adults earlier. Going past that range was not linked with a further drop in risk.

These are observational studies. They show a link between daily steps and health outcomes, not that a given number of steps causes a given result.

Does walking pace matter?

Less than you might expect. Once the researchers accounted for total daily steps, how fast people stepped did not show a consistent extra link with lower risk. The total number of steps mattered most.

How do steps fit with activity guidelines?

The World Health Organization recommends that adults get at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity physical activity a week, along with muscle-strengthening activity. Brisk walking counts toward those minutes, and your step count is an easy way to see how much of your day is spent moving.

How do you pick a step goal you will keep?

  1. Look at your average over the last one or two weeks, not your best day.
  2. Set the next goal a little above that average, for example 500 to 1,000 more steps a day.
  3. Keep it for a week or two, then check your average again before moving it.

A goal close to your real average is easier to reach on most days, and reaching it often is what builds the habit.
